POLENTA

I have made polenta nearly every week for twenty years and have changed only one thing about it~the pot. A wide, heavy pot cooks it faster and scorches it less than a tall one. For coarsely ground polenta {see Sources and Resources, here}, count on a full hour of intermittent stirring.

Salt it early. Polenta salted only at the end tastes flat no matter how much you add, and the grain needs the whole cooking time to take it in.

Wine: Merlot

FOR ABOUT 4-1/2 CUPS, OR 4 TO 8 SERVINGS:

5 cups water

1 cup coarsely ground polenta

About 2 teaspoons salt, or to taste

Unsalted butter, to taste {optional}

Bring the water to a boil in a wide, heavy pot. Add the salt, then rain in the polenta in a thin stream, whisking constantly so it does not clump.

Reduce the heat until the surface only just bubbles and cook, stirring every few minutes and scraping the bottom, until the grain no longer tastes raw and pulls away from the sides of the pot, about 1 hour. Beat in butter, if using, and taste again for salt.

Variation POLENTA with FRESH CORN

The corn goes in at the very end and stays raw enough to pop~cooked any longer, it goes starchy and disappears into the polenta instead of punctuating it.

FOR ABOUT 5 CUPS:

1 recipe polenta, made without butter and kept warm

About 1 cup fresh corn kernels {3 to 4 small ears} {see here}

Salt, to taste

Fold the corn into the hot polenta, cover, and leave off the heat for 2 minutes. Taste for salt and serve at once.

SOFT POLENTA with BRAISED GREENS

This is the looser, wetter polenta~closer to a porridge than a slab~and it wants greens cooked long enough to go silky. Bitter greens are best; the polenta underneath is sweet enough to carry them.

Serve it in shallow bowls and pour the greens and their liquid over the top rather than stirring them in.

FOR 4 SERVINGS:

1 recipe Polenta {see page 190}, made with 6 cups water

2 pounds escarole, chard, or dandelion greens, trimmed and coarsely chopped

1/4 cup mild-tasting olive oil

4 garlic cloves, thinly sliced

Salt and freshly cracked black pepper, to taste

Warm the olive oil and garlic in a wide pan over low heat until the garlic is fragrant and pale gold, about 3 minutes.

Add the greens in handfuls with the water still clinging to them, season with salt, cover, and braise over low heat until completely tender, 25 to 35 minutes. Add a splash of water if the pan goes dry.

Spoon the hot polenta into shallow bowls, top with the greens and their juices, and finish with pepper and a little more olive oil.
